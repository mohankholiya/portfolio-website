Turning fragmented CapEx buying into category leverage.

A capital programme tendered package by package leaks leverage at every award. Managing it as three categories — each matched to its own supply market — converted scattered tenders into roughly $4M of CapEx savings and a governance model the client kept.

IndustryOil & gas — upstream E&P
Engagement contextConsulting · Accenture, Indian E&P operator
RoleCapEx category & sourcing lead
Duration12 months

The situation

  • A large upstream capital programme was being sourced project by project, each package tendered in isolation.
  • There was no category strategy and no aggregated demand view, which left limited leverage against a concentrated contractor base.
  • Coordination had to arrive without loosening attention on technical requirements, quality and delivery schedule.

My contribution

Within Accenture's engagement team I led CapEx sourcing and category management across the major spend categories — building category strategies for EPC, surface facilities and drilling & completions; aggregating demand across projects; running sourcing end to end from qualification and RFx through evaluation, negotiation and award; and embedding category governance and supplier-management discipline in the client's own team.

The judgment that mattered

Leverage came from the structure of the process as much as from the size of the package. A concentrated market does not reward a bigger bidder list — it rewards disciplined stage-gates, which is why each category earned a different contracting approach rather than one tendering formula.

What the work delivered

  • Approximately $4M in CapEx savings delivered across the managed sourcing portfolio.
  • Category strategies established across EPC, surface facilities and drilling & completions, replacing project-by-project buying.
  • Demand aggregated across projects to build leverage against a concentrated supplier base.
  • Category playbooks and supplier-management routines embedded with the in-house team.
