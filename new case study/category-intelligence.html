Different markets deserve different sourcing strategies.

Lighting and E-STATCOM were being approached identically despite opposite supply dynamics. One intelligence framework applied to both showed why one should be competed hard and the other secured early — and what it would cost to get that backwards.

IndustryEnergy & utilities · industrial procurement
Engagement contextConsulting · energy utility
RoleCategory intelligence & market briefing
Duration8 weeks

The situation

  • Sourcing decisions were approaching on two categories that could hardly be less alike: LED luminaires, high-volume and commoditising, and E-STATCOM, engineered and supplier-concentrated.
  • There was no consolidated view of market size, growth or supplier concentration for either, and no visibility of what actually moved price.
  • Both were being approached with the same sourcing posture despite opposite supply dynamics.

My contribution

I led the category-intelligence assessment and the stakeholder briefings for both categories — defining one framework spanning market structure, cost drivers, price outlook and supply risk; building the cost-driver breakdowns and directional price outlook; mapping each supplier landscape; and translating the findings into a differentiated sourcing posture procurement and engineering could agree on.

The judgment that mattered

The shift that mattered was from describing a market to saying what procurement should do about it. Common headings kept the two categories comparable without implying they should be sourced the same way — which is the error the work existed to prevent.

What the work delivered

  • A single intelligence base holding market structure, cost drivers and price outlook for both categories in one comparable frame.
  • A differentiated posture: Lighting positioned for competitive leverage, E-STATCOM for strategic partnership and early commitment.
  • Cost-driver transparency that lets the client challenge supplier quotes on the inputs that actually move price.
  • Executive-ready briefings that aligned procurement and engineering ahead of the sourcing decisions.
