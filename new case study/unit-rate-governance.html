Finding the value hidden in contractor billing.

A ~$230M contractor programme was billing ~$5–6M a year above the assessment benchmark. The cause was not which contractors held the work — it was the absence of rate-book enforcement, which meant replacing them would have reproduced it.

IndustryEnergy & utilities — electric distribution
Engagement contextConsulting · major US electric utility
RoleIndependent commercial assessment (pre-RFP)
Duration10 weeks

The situation

  • A 2026 renewal of the overhead-construction contracts was approaching, against a unit-rate book that had not been refreshed in five years.
  • Ambiguous work descriptions and time-based billing made the commercial exposure real but unquantified, with no competitive-tension mechanism in the contract.
  • Any diagnosis had to pinpoint where governance failed across 482 rate-book items and six contractors without boiling the ocean.

My contribution

I led the assessment end to end — designing the three-layer method, analysing twelve months of approved spend covering ~$230M across roughly 565,000 transactions, 482 rate-book items and six contractors, quantifying above-market billing, isolating its structural root cause, and converting the diagnosis into a five-pillar contracting framework and phased roadmap tied to the RFP critical path.

The judgment that mattered

A rate comparison is only useful when the work and its measurement units are genuinely comparable. I weighted each contractor's rates by their own transaction volumes and held the finding together as one integrated opportunity rather than splitting it into a list of individually deniable line items.

What the work delivered

  • A ~$5–6M annual opportunity quantified against the assessment benchmark and shown to be addressable through a single structural lever.
  • Rate-book enforcement identified as that lever — avoiding a costly contractor reshuffle that would not have fixed the cause.
  • An RFP-ready toolkit: a five-pillar contract framework, an upgraded work-description standard and a standardised pricing template.
  • A sequenced roadmap tied to the renewal critical path, each reform mapped to a specific governance failure.
