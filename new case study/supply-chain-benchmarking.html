The right capacity, in the right parts of the function.

Headcount ratios would have called this supply-chain organisation efficient. Assessing workload role by role showed something more useful: a lean structure carrying peer-topping spend per person, with no headroom left for the growth it was being asked to absorb.

IndustryEnergy & utilities — regulated
Engagement contextConsulting · US energy utility
RoleOrganisational benchmarking & operating-model assessment
Duration12 weeks

The situation

  • Leadership needed to know whether the supply-chain organisation was structured to support current spend and planned growth.
  • Raw headcount comparisons risked obscuring real differences in role scope, process design and functional coverage between peers.
  • The objective was a fact base for workforce and operating-model decisions, not a headcount verdict.

My contribution

I led the benchmarking assessment across structure, role allocation and functional capacity — defining the peer-comparison framework, assessing spend supported per FTE across the organisation, reviewing coverage across sourcing, procurement operations, PMO/CoE and planning, and translating the findings into executive-level structural implications.

The judgment that mattered

High spend per person can mean a lean organisation or an overloaded one, and the two call for opposite responses. Reading the number alongside scope, service expectations and how execution work was distributed is what turned a benchmark into a recommendation.

What the work delivered

  • A structured fact base on supply-chain sizing relative to peers, usable directly in workforce planning.
  • Visibility into where spend responsibility concentrated across roles and where functional coverage thinned.
  • Lean enablement and the absence of a standalone planning function identified as the two structural gaps.
  • A prioritised reinforcement roadmap: clearer ownership of coordination-heavy work, execution support in procurement operations, incremental PMO/CoE capacity and formal planning capability.
